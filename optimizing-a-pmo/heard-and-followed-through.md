# Heard, and Followed Through

**Status:** 🚧 In progress

Optimizing a PMO depends on two groups feeling supported: the people who work in it, and the stakeholders it serves. Both need the same thing. When they tell you something, they need to see it lead somewhere. People who see their input acted on keep giving it, and keep doing their best work. People who talk and see nothing change stop talking, and the PMO loses the information it needs most.

This is mostly communication: more of it than feels necessary, clear, and above all followed through.

## The loop

Every piece of input goes around the same loop. The last step is the one most often skipped, and it is the one people remember.

1. **Listen.** Ask, and give people time to answer properly.
2. **Acknowledge.** Say what you heard, in their words, within two working days.
3. **Decide.** Do it, do part of it, or explain why not. A clear "not now, and here is why" is better than silence.
4. **Act.** Give each commitment an owner and a date.
5. **Close the loop.** Go back to the person and show them the result: "You told us this. Here is what changed."

## For the people in the PMO

Optimizing the office should make their work better, not just different.

- **Ask what would make their week better,** early and often, one to one. Act on the first few answers within the first month. Small, visible changes build the trust that larger ones need.
- **Take low-value work off their plate first.** Reports and meetings that no decision depends on are the easiest gift to give, and they free time for the work people joined to do.
- **Make it safe to raise yellow.** When someone brings bad news early, thank them and help. How the first few are received sets the pattern for everyone.
- **Set a fair load,** with a limit on how many programs or projects one person carries, and hold to it.
- **Show the path forward:** a development plan, a career ladder, and work that builds skills. People stay where they can see themselves growing.
- **Recognize the work,** in public and specifically: what the person did, and what it made possible.
- **Tell them first.** People in the PMO should hear about changes to their work from their own leader, before anyone else.

## For stakeholders

The same loop, at the scale of a partner group.

- **Hold listening sessions,** not only status meetings. Ask each group what is working, what is not, and what they need next. The questions in the [playbook's diagnosis](playbook.md#weeks-3-to-6-diagnose) are a good start.
- **Keep a "you said, we did" log.** Each item a stakeholder raised, what the PMO decided, and what happened. Publish it to them every month.
- **Commit to response times:** acknowledge within two working days, and give either an answer or a date for one.
- **Report back in their terms,** using the success measure each group gave you in the [stakeholder register](stakeholders.md).

## Communicate more than feels necessary

During change, people fill silence with their own story, and it is usually worse than the real one.

- **Say three things every time:** what is changing, what is not decided yet, and when it will be.
- **Use the same message for everyone,** so that no group hears a different version.
- **Repeat it across channels:** in meetings, in writing, and one to one. A message heard once is not yet understood.
- **No surprises.** Tell people before they could hear it from someone else.
- **Run it as a plan,** using the [Communications Plan Template](https://github.com/ChefPlex/tpm-templates/blob/main/communications-plan-template.md) for the cadence and the [Stakeholder Engagement Playbook](https://github.com/ChefPlex/tpm-templates/blob/main/stakeholder-engagement-playbook.md) for whose alignment you need.

## How we know it is working

Track these with the rest of [the measures](metrics.md), every quarter, as trends.

| Measure | How to measure |
|:---|:---|
| Follow-through | Share of raised items that reached step 5, with the person told the result |
| Time to acknowledge | Working days from input to acknowledgement |
| Time to close | Working days from input to a result the person was told about |
| Feeling heard | In the quarterly check, "When you raise something with the PMO, do you see it lead somewhere?", asked of people in the PMO and of each partner group |
| Staying and growing | Retention of the PMO's people, and moves into larger roles |
