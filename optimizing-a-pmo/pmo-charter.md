# The PMO Charter

**Status:** 🚧 In progress

A fill-in charter for the PMO itself. It follows the [Program Charter Template](https://github.com/ChefPlex/tpm-templates/blob/main/program-charter-template.md) section by section and adds what only a PMO needs: its services, what it will not do, its lever, its money ledger and when to change course on its own improvement plan. Lines in *italics* are illustrative examples. Replace every one.

Keep it to four pages, no longer than the charters the PMO asks programs to write. A short charter shows the PMO holds itself to the same standard.

## 1. Why this office exists

**The problem.** What the sponsor cannot do alone, in the sponsor's own words. *"We run 40 programs across eight engineering groups. I cannot see risk across them until it is already late, and every group reports status a different way."*

**What it is for.** One sentence. *"The PMO helps the business get the right work done when promised, by delegated oversight of portfolio risk, standard reporting and decisions brought early."*

**The mandate it is renewing.** What the office was originally set up to do, and how its role has shifted since. *"Created in 2022 to standardize reporting. Over time the role narrowed to collecting status, and teams now feel the reporting costs them more time than it saves."*

## 2. What the office delivers

**Services.** The few things the PMO actually does. Each one names who it is for. See [Project, Program and Portfolio](three-levels.md).

| Service | For whom | Level |
|:---|:---|:---|
| *Portfolio inventory, scoring and stop or start recommendations* | *Sponsor and governing body* | *Portfolio* |
| *Cross-program dependency and decision forum* | *Program managers* | *Program* |
| *Blocker removal within two working days* | *Project managers and engineering leads* | *Project* |
| *One standard status format* | *Everyone* | *All* |

**What the office will not do.** Write this list down. It is the part that builds trust fastest. *"We collect only the status that someone uses to make a decision. We do not approve work below tier 2. We do not own team delivery metrics."*

**Definition of success.** How the sponsor and the stakeholders will judge the office, in their terms. See [Stakeholders](stakeholders.md).

| Measure | Today | Target | Checked by |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| *Programs whose forecast lands within its range* | *[baseline]* | *[target]* | *Finance* |
| *Hours a week teams spend on PMO reporting* | *[baseline]* | *Lower* | *Engineering leads* |
| *Trust in the PMO on ability, benevolence and integrity* | *[survey baseline]* | *Up in every partner group* | *The quarterly check* |

## 3. Scope

**In scope.** Which work the PMO governs, by tier. See [Governance tiers](operating-model.md#governance-tiers). *"Tiers 1 and 2. Tier 3 is visible in the portfolio view only. Tier 4 is outside the PMO."*

**Out of scope.** *"Product prioritization, which stays with Product. Engineering staffing decisions, which stay with engineering leaders."*

## 4. How the office works

**The lever.** The one hard thing the PMO can do, written so it can be enforced. Keep it to one. *"No tier 1 commitment is accepted until its dependencies are signed by the teams that will deliver them. The portfolio review can stop or delay a tier 1 program."*

**Governance.** The tiers, and what each one gets.

**Mechanisms.** Every recurring meeting and report, each with an owner and an expiry date. See [Mechanisms](operating-model.md#mechanisms).

| Mechanism | Purpose | Owner | Expires |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| *Weekly delivery review* | *Decisions on blocked work* | *[name]* | *[date]* |
| *Monthly portfolio review* | *Stop, start, rebalance* | *[name]* | *[date]* |

**Reporting.** Red, amber and green are defined once, for every program. See the [Program Status Reporting Framework](https://github.com/ChefPlex/program-reporting-frameworks/blob/main/program-status-reporting-framework.md).

**Milestones for the office.** For an improvement plan, the [90-day playbook](playbook.md): stop list done, one major program unblocked, this charter signed. Then months 4 to 12.

## 5. Resources

**People.** The PMO's own staff, by role. *"Head of PMO, four program managers, one portfolio analyst."*

**Budget.** The office's fully loaded annual cost. It is the first line of the money ledger.

**The money ledger.** What the office returns, agreed with Finance and reviewed every quarter. See [The money ledger](operating-model.md#the-money-ledger).

| Line | This quarter |
|:---|:---|
| PMO cost | *[amount]* |
| Capacity freed by stopping or merging work | *[amount]* |
| Cost of delay on the five biggest programs | *[amount]* |
| Spend avoided | *[amount]* |
| Finance and audit data still flowing | *[yes or no]* |

## 6. Stakeholders and communications

**RACI for the office's own decisions.** Who decides what. See the [RACI templates](https://github.com/ChefPlex/tpm-templates/blob/main/raci-template-waterfall.md).

| Decision | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted | Informed |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| *Stop or delay a tier 1 program* | *Head of PMO* | *Sponsor* | *Program owner, Finance* | *Affected teams* |
| *Change the PMO's services* | *Head of PMO* | *Sponsor* | *Partner groups* | *Everyone* |

**Stakeholder register.** Each partner group, its win, concern and currency, and the PMO person who owns the relationship. See [Stakeholders](stakeholders.md).

**Sponsor access.** A standing slot with the sponsor, at least every two weeks. Written down, because a 2010 study found that PMOs lost this access when they were reorganized.

## 7. Risks, assumptions and dependencies

**Risks.** *"The sponsor changes. Partner groups keep their own tracking alongside the standard report, and the two drift apart. Finance depends on data from a report we plan to stop."*

**Assumptions.** *"Finance will agree the money ledger. Engineering leaders will name a dependency owner for each tier 1 program."*

**Dependencies.** *"The tracker data is accurate enough to report from. Sample it before relying on it."*

## 8. Compliance and reviews

Which data the PMO produces that someone else depends on: capitalization of software work, audit evidence, regulatory approvals. *"Time data feeds capitalization; it continues unchanged."* Check this before stopping any report.

## 9. When to change course, and review

**When to change course.** At month six, change course if any is true. See [the playbook](playbook.md#when-to-change-course).
- The lever is still unsigned, or the sponsor no longer attends the portfolio review.
- The pilots' forecasts are no closer to actuals than the baseline was.
- Teams report no drop in reporting load, measured in hours.
- The money ledger shows no capacity freed and no spend avoided.
- Trust has fallen on any of the three traits in two or more partner groups.

**Review.** The sponsor and the head of PMO review this charter on the date above, and whenever the sponsor changes. A change of sponsor resets the clock.
