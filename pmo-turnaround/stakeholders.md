# Stakeholders

**Status:** 🚧 In progress

Stakeholders are the reason for the turnaround. A PMO works for the business. Its job is to create business value by putting the skills of its project, program and portfolio managers to work on the business's goals. If the people it serves do not see that value, the PMO has failed, however good its methods are.

## What value means here

PMI defines business value as "the net quantifiable benefit derived from a business endeavor," which may be tangible, intangible or both, and a benefit as a gain "realized by the organization and other stakeholders" (*PMI Lexicon of Project Management Terms*). Both definitions point the same way. Value is what the stakeholders receive, not what the PMO produces. A report, a template or a meeting has value only if someone outside the PMO uses it to get something done.

So the turnaround is judged by stakeholders, not by the PMO. The research agrees on where the pressure comes from: in a study of PMO changes, problems with collaboration and accountability with stakeholders were among the strongest drivers of reorganizing a PMO, and organizational politics played a significant role (Aubry, Hobbs, Müller and Blomquist, PMI Research Conference, 2010).

## Who they are and what each needs

| Stakeholder | What they need from the PMO | How they would know it is working |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Executive sponsor** (CTO, CIO) | Risk across the portfolio seen and managed before it reaches them; decisions brought to them ready to make | Fewer surprises; the decisions they make are the ones only they can make |
| **Business and product leaders** | Their priorities delivered when promised; honest trade-offs when everything cannot be done | Dates hold; when they do not, they hear early, with options |
| **Engineering leaders** | Blockers cleared, dependencies brokered, less reporting overhead | Their teams spend more time building and less time reporting |
| **Finance** | Reliable spend, capitalization and forecast data | The numbers reconcile; the money ledger shows capacity freed and spend avoided |
| **Security, risk, audit and compliance** | Their obligations scheduled and evidenced, not discovered late | Required work lands on time, with evidence that holds up |
| **Operations** | Working handovers, with someone owning each benefit | Nothing goes live that operations cannot run |
| **The PMO's own people** | A clear mandate and work that is respected | They can say what the business gets from them, and the business agrees |

## How to work with them in a turnaround

The method for mapping and moving stakeholders is already written up in the [Stakeholder Engagement Playbook](https://github.com/ChefPlex/tpm-templates/blob/main/stakeholder-engagement-playbook.md): who has a stake, where each sits on power and interest, and each one's win, concern and currency. What follows is what changes when the program being rescued is the PMO itself.

- **Start with them, not the methodology.** Run the separate conversations in [the playbook](playbook.md#weeks-3-to-6-diagnose) and write down, in each group's own words, the problem it needs solved.
- **Find out which kind of trust is missing,** for each group: ability, benevolence or integrity ([What the Evidence Says](evidence.md#trust)). The repair is different for each.
- **Keep a stakeholder register at the portfolio level:** who each group is, its win, concern and currency, how it measures value, and who in the PMO owns the relationship. Run the cadence through a [communications plan](https://github.com/ChefPlex/tpm-templates/blob/main/communications-plan-template.md).
- **Name a benefit owner on every program,** a person outside the PMO who will say whether the benefit arrived. Without one, nobody can say whether the work was worth doing.
- **Report in their terms.** Each group gets the view it needs to make its decisions, not one dashboard for everyone. The [three levels](three-levels.md) set what each audience needs.
- **Ask them, on a schedule.** A short quarterly check with each group, on its own measure and on the three trust traits, is the turnaround's real scorecard.
- **Guard the access to the top.** The same 2010 study found that PMOs gained importance after a reorganization but lost access to upper management. Keep the sponsor's standing slot.
